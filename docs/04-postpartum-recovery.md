# 04. Postpartum Recovery & Care 🌸

A dedicated physical and emotional recovery guide for mum, with actionable protocols and a clear "watchdog" framework for partner.

---

## 🩹 1. Physical Healing & Comfort Toolkit

Birth is an endurance athletic event. Whether birth is physiological, assisted, or surgical, mum’s body requires deliberate rehabilitation.

### 1. Perineal Care & Wound Management
- **The Peri Bottle:** Fill the upside-down peri bottle with warm tap water. Squirt over the perineum *while* urinating to dilute urine and prevent stinging. Pat gently dry with soft toilet paper or cotton cloths—never wipe.
- **Cooling "Padsicles":** Spread witch hazel or pure aloe vera onto maternity pads and freeze them for 30 minutes before wearing. Provides immediate swelling and bruising relief.
- **Underwear:** High-waisted disposable underwear or oversized cotton knickers that sit well above any potential C-section incision or sensitive pubic bone areas.
- **Lochia (Postnatal Bleeding):** Bleeding continues for 3 to 6 weeks as the placental site heals:
  - *Days 1–4:* Bright red, heavy (like a very heavy period), may have small clots (size of a 50p coin).
  - *Days 5–14:* Brownish/pinkish, lighter flow.
  - *Weeks 2–6:* Yellowish-white discharge, tapering off.
  - ⚠️ **Red Flag:** Soaking through more than one heavy maternity pad per hour, passing clots larger than a golf ball, or foul-smelling discharge. Call NNUH Maternity Triage immediately.

### 2. C-Section Recovery Contingency
If a Caesarean birth occurs:
- Keep the incision line clean and bone dry. Pat dry gently after showering.
- Wear loose, high-waisted clothing that does not rub against the dressing.
- No heavy lifting for the first 6 weeks (nothing heavier than your baby in your arms).
- Roll onto your side and use your arms to push yourself up when getting out of bed to protect your abdominal wall.

## ⏰ 2. Partner-Managed Pain Relief Schedule

> **Partner's Duty:** Do not wait for mum to ask for pain relief or wait until she is in agonizing pain. Set timers on your phone and bring the tablets with a glass of water and a light snack on schedule for the first 5–7 days.

| Medication | Standard NHS Adult Dose | Frequency | Timing / Rules |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Paracetamol** | 1000mg (2x 500mg tablets) | Every 4–6 hours | Max 4000mg in 24 hours. Safe while breastfeeding. |
| **Ibuprofen** | 400mg (2x 200mg tablets) | Every 6–8 hours | Max 1200mg in 24 hours. Always take with food or milk. |

### Sample Staggered Schedule (Keeps Pain Constantly Managed)
- **06:00 AM:** Paracetamol (1000mg)
- **08:30 AM (Breakfast):** Ibuprofen (400mg)
- **12:00 PM:** Paracetamol (1000mg)
- **02:30 PM (Lunch/Snack):** Ibuprofen (400mg)
- **06:00 PM:** Paracetamol (1000mg)
- **08:30 PM (Dinner/Snack):** Ibuprofen (400mg)
- **11:00 PM (Before Sleep Shift):** Paracetamol (1000mg)

*Note: Uterine cramping ("after-pains") is normal and especially noticeable during breastfeeding as oxytocin contracts the uterus back to its pre-pregnancy size. Ibuprofen is particularly effective for this.*

## 🧠 3. Emotional Wellbeing & Partner "Watchdog" Role

Postnatal hormones plunge dramatically. Mum may not recognize when she is struggling. Partner acts as the loving, watchful guardian.

### 1. The Daily 5-Minute Evening Check-in
Every evening while baby is settled or resting on partner's chest, ask one simple, non-judgmental question:
> *"How is your head today on a scale from 1 to 10?"*
- **1–4:** Struggling / overwhelmed. Partner takes over chores and baby duty completely; focus on food, rest, and comfort.
- **5–7:** Treading water, normal postpartum exhaustion. Validate and support.
- **8–10:** Feeling steady and positive. Celebrate the win!

### 2. "Baby Blues" vs Postnatal Depression/Anxiety (PND / PNA)

| Feature | Baby Blues (Normal & Temporary) | Postnatal Depression / Anxiety (Needs Support) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Timing** | Days 3 to 10 post-birth. | Emerges anytime in first year, often weeks 2–8. |
| **Symptoms** | Tearfulness, emotional sensitivity, feeling tired and weepy over sweet or minor things. | Persistent numbness, intense dread, constant crying, inability to feel joy. |
| **Sleep** | Able to sleep when baby sleeps and opportunity arises. | Severe insomnia: mind races and unable to sleep even when baby is sound asleep. |
| **Duration** | Naturally resolves within 10–14 days as hormones stabilize. | Persists beyond 2 weeks or worsens over time. |

### 3. Red Flags & Urgent Contacts
If mum experiences persistent intrusive thoughts, extreme detachment, panic attacks, or thoughts of self-harm:
- **GP Surgery:** Call and request an urgent same-day postnatal review.
- **NHS 111 (Option 2):** 24/7 dedicated mental health triage.
- **PANDAS Foundation Helpline:** **0808 1961 776** (Free UK helpline, Mon–Sun 10am–10pm).
- **Maternal Mental Health Service (Norfolk):** Accessible via referral from midwife, health visitor, or GP.

## 🤱 4. Breast Care, Engorgement & Mastitis Prevention

### 1. The Milk Transition (Days 1–5)
- **Days 1–3 (Colostrum):** Thick, golden, nutrient-dense "first milk". Measured in teaspoons, which is exactly what baby's marble-sized stomach needs. Frequent feeds stimulate production.
- **Days 3–5 (Mature Milk "Coming In"):** Breasts may feel suddenly hot, firm, heavy, and swollen (engorgement).

### 2. Soothing Engorgement Protocol
- **Before Feed:** Take a warm shower or apply a warm flannel for 2–3 minutes to stimulate the letdown reflex. Perform gentle "reverse pressure softening" (pushing gently back towards the chest wall around the areola for 60 seconds) so the nipple softens for baby to latch.
- **During Feed:** Ensure deep asymmetrical latch (chin pressed into breast, nose free, wide open mouth like a yawn).
- **After Feed:** Apply cold gel packs or chilled clean savoy cabbage leaves wrapped in a light cloth for 10–15 minutes to reduce tissue swelling.

### 3. Nipple Care Routine
- Wear **Silverettes** (pure silver nursing cups) inside the nursing bra between feeds. Silver has natural antimicrobial and healing properties.
- Dab a drop of expressed breast milk or pure lanolin balm onto the nipple after nursing and allow to air-dry.

### 4. Mastitis Warning Signs & Rapid Response
Mastitis is an inflammation/infection of breast tissue caused by a blocked milk duct or bacterial entry:
- **Symptoms:** A hot, red, tender wedge or streak on the breast, accompanied by flu-like shivers, body aches, and a fever above 38°C.
- **Action Plan:**
  1. **Do NOT stop nursing:** Feed frequently from the affected breast or gently express to keep milk moving.
  2. **Anti-inflammatory:** Take 400mg Ibuprofen regularly to reduce swelling.
  3. **Cold Compresses:** Use ice packs between feeds to calm the inflammation (avoid vigorous deep massage, which can damage delicate breast tissue).
  4. **Call GP:** If fever or red patch does not improve within 12–24 hours, call your GP or NHS 111 for a course of antibiotics (Flucloxacillin is standard and safe while nursing).
