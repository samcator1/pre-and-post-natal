# 06. Practical Support & Visitor Guide 🤝

A no-fuss, practical guide to welcoming family and friends without becoming overwhelmed, putting willing helpers to work, and smoothly ending visits without awkwardness.

---

## 🛑 The Three Golden Rules

1. **Hospital is for Us:** Zero visitors at Norfolk and Norwich Hospital. Family and friends meet baby once we are settled in our own home.
2. **Wash Hands Immediately:** All visitors wash their hands thoroughly with warm water and soap as soon as they step through the front door.
3. **No Kissing the Baby:** Absolutely no kissing baby on the face, head, or hands (to protect against neonatal herpes/cold sores and RSV). Anyone feeling even slightly unwell or coughing stays home.

---

## ⏱️ Rule 1: How to Handle Visitors

Visits should bring calm and relief, not stress.

- **Set Expectations Before Arrival:** When scheduling, give an explicit start and end window:  
  *"We’d love to see you on Tuesday between 2:00 PM and 2:45 PM for a quick cuddle before baby's next feed!"*
- **Cap Visits at 45 Minutes Max:** Newborns feed every 2–3 hours. A 45-minute visit allows guests to meet baby, have a cup of tea, and leave before the next cluster feed or nappy explosion.
- **Limit to One Group Per Day:** Never schedule back-to-back visitors. You need uninterrupted recovery time in between.
- **Mum Decides Baby-Holding:** If baby is sleeping peacefully on mum or in the crib, they do not need to be picked up or passed around like a pass-the-parcel.

---

## 🛠️ Rule 2: How to Get Them to Help

People genuinely want to help, but they rarely know what to do unless you give them a specific job. Treat their visit as a supportive partnership!

### The "Visitor Admission Ticket"
Before a guest arrives, ask them to pick up one simple thing:
- *"Could you grab a pint of whole milk and a loaf of bread on your way?"*
- *"If you're bringing food, a ready-to-heat pasta bake or curry that we can pop in the fridge would be amazing!"*

### Specific Jobs to Give Guests While They Are in the House
Never play the gracious host waiting on guests with tea and biscuits. Instead, give them high-value tasks:
- **"The Tea & Dishwasher Swap":** *"The kettle and mugs are right there—please make yourself at home and put on a brew for everyone! While the kettle boils, would you mind emptying the clean dishwasher?"*
- **"The Shower Sitter":** Hand baby to the grandparent/guest: *"Would you mind cuddling baby on the sofa for 20 minutes? That gives mum the perfect window to take a long, hot shower and wash her hair."*
- **"The Dog Walker / Rubbish Runner":** *"Would you mind taking the dog for a quick 15-minute loop around the block or taking that bag of recycling out to the green bin as you leave?"*

---

## 🚪 Rule 3: How to Get Them to Leave (Without Awkwardness)

Ending a visit shouldn't feel confrontational. Partner plays the role of the **"Friendly Timekeeper"** so mum never has to feel uncomfortable or rude.

### Strategy A: The "Feed & Nap" Transition (Mum's Exit)
Around the 35–40 minute mark, mum stands up with baby:
> *"Baby is showing hunger cues, so I'm going to take them upstairs into the quiet for a feed and a nap. It was so wonderful seeing you both!"*  
*(Mum goes upstairs; visit is officially over).*

### Strategy B: The Partner Doorway Sweep
Once mum goes upstairs (or while baby is settled), partner immediately stands up, smiles, and starts wrapping up:
> *"Right, I promised to get mum and baby down for some rest now before the next cluster feed. Let me grab your coats and walk you to the door!"*
- Hand them their coats and bags with a warm smile.
- Walk them right out to the front doorstep.
- Thank them warmly for their visit and for bringing food/helping.

### Strategy C: The Pre-Programmed Alarm
If you have visitors who notoriously ignore social cues:
- Set a subtle chime/alarm on partner's phone for 40 minutes into the visit.
- When it rings: *"Ah, that's our reminder—midwife/health visitor check is coming up / it's time for baby's medicine and sleep window. Let me grab your shoes!"*

## 📱 Quick Copy-Paste Message Templates

### 1. Baby Arrival Announcement (Setting Gentle Boundaries)
> *"Baby [Name] has arrived safely! Mum and baby are both doing brilliantly, and we are completely in love. We are taking the first few days to rest, recover, and bond in our quiet bubble at home. We can't wait to introduce you all once we're settled in. We'll be in touch soon with photos and to arrange short visits!"*

### 2. Gentle Decline / Raincheck
> *"Thank you so much for thinking of us! We’ve had a rather sleepless night and today needs to be a quiet rest day for mum and baby to recharge. Can we pencil you in for a quick visit next week instead?"*
