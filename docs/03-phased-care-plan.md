# 03. Phased Care Plan (Days, Weeks & Months) 🗓️

A holistic parent-and-baby operational roadmap covering the transition from hospital homecoming through the fourth trimester and into the third month.

---

## 🐣 Phase 1: The First 72 Hours (Homecoming & Survival)

The focus of the first 3 days is rest, establishing feeding, and recovering from birth.

### 1. Mum’s "Recovery Nest" Setup
Before sitting down, set up a dedicated resting station in the living room and bedroom so mum doesn't need to stand up constantly:
- **Arm’s Reach Station:** Large insulated water bottle (with straw), high-protein snacks (nuts, flapjacks), phone charger with long cable, lip balm, and TV remote.
- **Nursing Caddy:** Breastfeeding pillow, Haakaa silicone collector, silver cups, lanolin balm, and 2–3 muslin cloths.
- **Nappy Station:** Downstairs changing caddy with nappies, cotton wool, warm water flask, and barrier cream.

### 2. Output & Feeding Benchmarks
Newborn stomachs start the size of a marble (5–7ml on Day 1), expanding to a walnut on Day 3. Track outputs on an app (like Huckleberry) or a paper chart on the fridge:

| Day | Expected Wet Nappies | Expected Dirty Nappies | Stool Appearance & Notes |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Day 1** | 1–2 wet | 1 or more dirty | **Meconium:** Thick, black, sticky tar-like stool. Normal and expected. |
| **Day 2** | 2–3 wet | 1–2 dirty | Transitional: Dark green/brown, softening. |
| **Day 3** | 3–4 wet | 2–3 dirty | Transitional: Greenish-yellow. Mum's mature milk begins coming in. |
| **Day 4** | 4–5 wet | 2–3 dirty | Turning golden yellow. Baby feeds 8–12 times per 24 hours. |
| **Day 5+** | 5–6+ heavy wet | 2–4+ dirty | **Yellow & seedy:** Classic mustard-yellow breastfed stool. |

### 3. NHS Clinical Checkpoints
- **Day 1–2 Home Visit:** Community midwife visits at home to check mum’s lochia/bleeding, perineum, baby’s skin colour (checking for jaundice), and umbilical cord stump.
- **Day 5 Midwife Visit:** 
  - **Heel Prick (Blood Spot) Test:** Screened for 9 rare conditions. (Partner tip: give baby a warm cuddle or nurse right before/during the heel prick to comfort them).
  - **Weight Check:** Weight drop of up to 7–10% of birth weight is common in the first few days. Midwife checks baby is starting to regain weight.

### 4. Day 3–4 Hormonal Plunge ("Baby Blues")
- Around Day 3 to 5, mum's estrogen and progesterone drop rapidly while prolactin surges to bring milk in.
- **Symptoms:** Sudden tearfulness, feeling overwhelmed by minor things, emotional vulnerability.
- **Partner Action:** Validate her feelings without trying to "fix" them. Offer hugs, tea, snacks, and reassurance: *"You are doing an incredible job. Your hormones are recalibrating, and this is completely normal."*

## 🌙 Phase 2: Weeks 1 to 4 (The Fourth Trimester)

The primary objective is protecting maternal recovery and managing sleep deprivation as a collaborative team.

### 1. Split Night Shift Strategy ("Feed & Pass")
Uninterrupted sleep in blocks of 4–5 hours is what preserves sanity and emotional regulation.

```
       9:00 PM                 2:00 AM                 7:00 AM
          │                       │                       │
          ▼                       ▼                       ▼
┌───────────────────┐   ┌───────────────────┐   ┌───────────────────┐
│  PARTNER SHIFT    │   │     MUM SHIFT     │   │   MORNING RESET   │
│  (Mum sleeps in   │   │  (Partner sleeps  │   │  Partner makes    │
│  separate bed)    │   │  in separate bed) │   │  breakfast/coffee │
└───────────────────┘   └───────────────────┘   └───────────────────┘
```

- **Shift 1 (9:00 PM – 2:00 AM) — Partner On-Duty:**
  - Mum sleeps in the spare bedroom / undisturbed with earplugs.
  - Partner manages baby in living room or bedroom.
  - **When baby cues for milk:** Partner brings baby to mum in bed. Mum nurses lying down or propped up. Once baby finishes, partner immediately takes baby back, burps them, changes nappy, and swaddles/settles baby so mum falls straight back to sleep.
- **Shift 2 (2:00 AM – 7:00 AM) — Mum On-Duty:**
  - Partner moves to the spare bedroom and sleeps undisturbed for 5 solid hours.
  - Mum manages baby from the bedside Next2Me crib.
- **7:00 AM Morning Reset:**
  - Partner wakes up refreshed, takes baby, makes coffee/tea and breakfast for mum, and lets mum nap for another hour or take a long shower.

### 2. "Chore Triage" & Minimum Viable Household
During weeks 1 to 4, drop all perfectionism. The **only** 3 non-negotiables are:
1. Baby is fed, warm, and safe.
2. Mum is nourished, hydrated, and resting.
3. Clean dishes and baby clothes cycle through the wash.

- **Meals:** Rely 100% on pre-cooked freezer meals (cottage pies, stews, curries) and ready-to-heat meals brought by visitors.
- **Everything Else:** Dusting, lawn mowing, deep cleaning, and social obligations are placed strictly on pause.

### 3. Daily Partner 45-Minute Walk
- Once mum is resting comfortably, partner places baby in the lie-flat pram or ergonomic baby carrier and goes for a 45-minute neighbourhood walk.
- **Benefits:**
  - Baby gets fresh air and soothing motion.
  - Partner gets fresh air, exercise, and dedicated bonding time.
  - Mum gets 45 minutes of guaranteed silent house time to take a peaceful bath, nap, or just breathe without listening out for cries.

## 🌿 Phase 3: Months 1 to 3 (Establishing Rhythms)

As baby grows out of the fragile newborn stage, alertness increases and predictable patterns begin to emerge.

### 1. Wake Windows & Preventing Overtiredness
Newborns cannot stay awake for long without their nervous system becoming overstimulated (leading to frantic crying and difficulty settling):

| Baby's Age | Approximate Wake Window | Sleep Cues to Watch For |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **0–4 Weeks** | 45–60 minutes | Staring into distance, yawning, red eyebrows, jerky movements. |
| **4–8 Weeks** | 60–75 minutes | Turning head away from faces/lights, frowning, rubbing eyes. |
| **8–12 Weeks** | 75–90 minutes | Fussiness, clinginess, yawning. |

**The Golden Rule:** At the first sleep cue, begin winding down (dim lights, swaddle, white noise, gentle shushing/swaying) rather than waiting for tears.

### 2. Taming the Evening "Witching Hour" (Weeks 4–8)
- Many babies experience a daily fussy period (usually between 5:00 PM and 10:00 PM) where they cluster feed and cry inconsolably. This peaks around week 6 and naturally subsides by week 10–12.
- **Survival Toolkit:**
  - **Skin-to-skin:** Strip baby down to nappy and place on partner’s bare chest.
  - **Movement outside:** Take baby outside into the cool evening air for 5 minutes (often acts like an instant reset button).
  - **Warm bath or running water:** Let baby listen to running water from the bathroom tap.
  - **Paced Cluster Feeding:** Support mum with endless water and snacks on the sofa.

### 3. Transitioning When Partner Returns to Work
When statutory paternity or annual leave ends:
- **Morning Handoff (7:00 AM – 8:00 AM):** Partner handles the morning nappy, dresses baby, and makes mum a substantial breakfast + fills two large water bottles before heading to work.
- **Evening Reunion (5:30 PM):** Partner walks in the door and immediately takes baby into their arms: *"I've got baby; go take 30 minutes for yourself."*
- **Shared Dinner:** Simple slow-cooker or prepped meals eaten together while baby naps or sits in the bouncer.

### 4. NHS Health Milestones in Months 1–3
- **Day 10–14:** Community Midwife officially discharges care and hands over to the local **Health Visitor** (first home review).
- **6–8 Weeks:** **GP 6–8 Week Check** (full physical exam for baby: hips, heart, eyes, testicles/genitalia, head circumference, weight; plus maternal postnatal check).
- **8 Weeks:** **First NHS Immunisations** (6-in-1, Rotavirus oral drops, MenB injection). *Administer Infant Paracetamol (Calpol) immediately after appointment to prevent MenB fever.*
- **12 Weeks:** **Second NHS Immunisations** (6-in-1, Rotavirus, Pneumococcal).
- **16 Weeks:** **Third NHS Immunisations** (6-in-1, MenB).
