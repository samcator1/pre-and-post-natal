## 🌟 Birth Preferences Summary (For Attending Midwives & Doctors)

> **Dear Midwifery and Obstetric Team,**  
> Thank you for supporting us during the birth of our first baby. We trust your professional expertise and care. Our goal is a calm, positive, and safe birth experience. Below are our core preferences. Please discuss options with us before any procedures using the **BRAIN** framework.

### 1. Environment & Room Setup
- **Lighting:** Dimmed lighting throughout labour and birth (please keep overhead examination lights off unless clinically required).
- **Audio:** We will have our own birth playlist / ambient sound machine playing.
- **Birth Pool:** We **do not** wish to use a birth pool for labour or delivery.
- **Atmosphere:** Calm, quiet voices; please avoid interrupting mum during active contractions.
- **Students:** We welcome student midwives and doctors, provided they introduce themselves and ask politely beforehand.

### 2. Communication & Partner's Role
- **Advocate & Gatekeeper:** Partner is mum’s primary support and advocate. Please direct logistical and non-urgent questions to partner first so mum can stay focused in her zone.
- **Informed Consent (BRAIN Framework):** For any proposed intervention, please give us a moment to discuss:
  - **B**enefits: *Why is this recommended right now?*
  - **R**isks: *What are the risks of doing this?*
  - **A**lternatives: *What else can we try first?*
  - **I**ntuition: *How does mum feel about it?*
  - **N**othing / Next: *What happens if we wait 30–60 minutes?*
- **Baby's Sex:** If a surprise, mum and partner would love to announce the sex themselves at birth!

### 3. Pain Management Approach
We are taking a **progressive, tiered ladder approach**:
1. **Tier 1 (First Line / Comfort Measures):**
   - Rhythmic breathing & relaxation techniques.
   - Freedom of movement, gentle rocking on the birthing ball, and walking.
   - TENS machine applied early in labour.
   - Partner massage, counter-pressure on lower back, and cold flannel forehead presses.
2. **Tier 2 (Inhaled Analgesia):**
   - Entonox (Gas & Air) introduced as contractions intensify.
3. **Tier 3 (Medical Relief if Needed):**
   - If labour is prolonged, exhausting, or pain becomes unmanageable, we are open to discussing medical relief (Pethidine / Diamorphine or an Epidural on Delivery Suite).
   - Please support mum with whatever decision she feels is right in the moment without pressure either way.

### 4. Labour Progression & Monitoring
- **Fetal Monitoring:** We prefer **intermittent monitoring** (Doppler / Pinard) where clinically appropriate so mum can remain mobile and out of bed.
- **Vaginal Examinations:** Please keep vaginal checks to a minimum, ensure mum's explicit consent before each check, and explain what is being assessed.
- **Membranes & Augmentation:** We prefer membranes to rupture spontaneously. Please discuss with us before artificial rupture of membranes (ARM) or Syntocinon drips.

### 5. Pushing & Delivery
- **Positions:** Freedom to push in upright, gravity-assisted positions (e.g. on all-fours, kneeling over the raised head of the bed, or side-lying). We wish to avoid lying flat on the back.
- **Directed Pushing:** Mum prefers to follow her body's natural urges (physiological pushing) rather than counting or holding breath, with gentle guidance from the midwife as baby crowns.
- **Perineal Care:** Warm compresses on the perineum during crowning. We prefer a natural tear over an episiotomy. Episiotomy only if baby is in distress or if required for an instrumental delivery.

### 6. Third Stage & Immediate Post-Birth (The Golden Hour)
- **Placenta Delivery:** **Active management** preferred (routine intramuscular injection of Syntometrine/oxytocin in the thigh to reduce postpartum haemorrhage risk).
- **Cord Clamping:** **Delayed Cord Clamping (DCC)**—please wait at least 2–3 minutes or until the umbilical cord has completely stopped pulsing and gone white before clamping.
- **Cutting the Cord:** **Partner** wishes to cut the umbilical cord once clamped.
- **Skin-to-Skin:** Immediate, uninterrupted skin-to-skin contact on mum's bare chest for at least the first hour (warm towel over baby; defer weighing, measuring, and wiping until after the first cuddle and feed).
- **Vitamin K:** We consent to the single intramuscular **Vitamin K injection** for baby after the first cuddle.

## ⚠️ Contingencies & Assisted / Surgical Birth Preferences

If labour requires medical assistance or transfer to theatre:

### Assisted Delivery (Ventouse / Forceps)
- Full explanation of why it is needed, with adequate pain relief (local anaesthetic / pudendal block or epidural top-up).
- Partner stays by mum's head offering calm encouragement.
- Baby brought straight to mum's chest as soon as safely possible.

### Caesarean Section ("Gentle C-Section" Protocol)
- **Partner Presence:** Partner to remain seated right next to mum's head throughout the entire procedure.
- **Atmosphere:** Please keep theatre chatter low and allow our background music/playlist to play.
- **Visuals:** Please lower the surgical screen at the moment of delivery so we can see our baby being born.
- **Sex Announcement:** Partner to look and tell mum the baby's sex.
- **Immediate Skin-to-Skin:** Baby placed skin-to-skin directly on mum's chest in theatre while the surgical team completes the closure. If mum feels unwell or unavailable, baby to go immediately skin-to-skin on partner's bare chest.

## 🎒 Hospital Bag Packing Checklist (3-Bag System)

Organise into three clearly labelled bags so partner can grab exactly what is needed without rummaging:

### Bag 1: Labour & Delivery (Take in immediately)
- [ ] Maternity notes / Badger Notes app logged in on phone
- [ ] TENS machine (with fresh batteries + spare pack of pads)
- [ ] Labour clothes (loose dark oversized T-shirt / nightdress / birthing gown)
- [ ] Warm fluffy socks + slip-on sliders/slippers
- [ ] Lip balm (essential with Gas & Air, which dries out lips)
- [ ] Hair ties, hair clips, and headband
- [ ] Insulated water bottle with straw (easy for partner to hold while mum rests)
- [ ] High-energy snacks (jelly babies, flapjacks, nuts, dried fruit) + isotonic sports drinks
- [ ] Portable phone charger (power bank) + extra-long charging cables
- [ ] Portable Bluetooth speaker / sound machine
- [ ] Flannel / small washcloth (for cold forehead compresses)

### Bag 2: Postnatal Ward Stay (Mum & Baby)
- **For Mum:**
  - [ ] 2x front-opening nursing nighties or button-up pyjamas (loose, soft cotton)
  - [ ] 1x comfortable going-home outfit (maternity leggings / joggers and loose top)
  - [ ] 2x nursing bras (non-wired, comfortable)
  - [ ] 8–10x high-waisted disposable underwear or dark oversized cotton knickers
  - [ ] 2x packs of maternity pads (heavy flow, ultra-cushioned)
  - [ ] Peri bottle (upside-down ergonomic design)
  - [ ] Silver nursing cups (Silverettes) + pure lanolin nipple balm
  - [ ] Toiletries: travel shampoo, body wash, toothbrush, toothpaste, deodorant, dry shampoo
  - [ ] Dark bath towel (hospital towels are often small and rough)
- **For Baby:**
  - [ ] 4x newborn sleepsuits (two-way zips with built-in scratch mitts)
  - [ ] 4x newborn short-sleeve bodysuits / vests
  - [ ] 2x 0–3 month sleepsuits (in case baby is 8.5+ lbs!)
  - [ ] 1x pack newborn nappies (Size 0 or 1)
  - [ ] 1x pack cotton wool pads or WaterWipes (pure water)
  - [ ] 2x 100% cotton cellular blankets
  - [ ] 3x soft muslin cloths
  - [ ] 1x soft cotton baby hat (for going home) + 1x knitted cardigan

### Bag 3: Partner & Going-Home (Can be left in car boot initially)
- [ ] Change of clothes + spare T-shirt + deodorant + toothbrush
- [ ] Coins/card for NNUH car parking machines and cafeteria/vending
- [ ] Extra snacks and drinks for partner
- [ ] Infant car seat (properly fitted to ISOFIX base or belted in before travel)
- [ ] Warm car blanket for baby over the car seat straps (never under the harness)
- [ ] Empty laundry bag for dirty clothes
