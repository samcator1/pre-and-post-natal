Birth Plan & Preferences

Norfolk and Norwich University Hospital (NNUH) Delivery Suite / MLBU.

Letter to Attending Midwives & Obstetric Team

Thank you for supporting us during the birth of our first baby. We trust your professional clinical expertise. Our goal is a calm, positive, and safe birth. Below are our core preferences. Please discuss procedures with us using the BRAIN framework.

🌿 Environment & Senses

  • Lighting: Keep lights dimmed throughout labour and delivery.
  • Sound: Ambient birth playlist playing via speaker.
  • Birth Pool: We do not wish to use a birth pool.
  • Calm Atmosphere: Quiet voices; avoid interrupting mum during active contractions.
  • Students: Student midwives/doctors welcome if introduced politely beforehand.

🛡️ Partner Advocacy & BRAIN

Partner is mum's primary advocate and gatekeeper for non-urgent questions.

Benefits — Why is this needed now?
Risks — What are the trade-offs?
Alternatives — What else can we try first?
Intuition — How does mum feel?
Nothing / Next — Can we wait 30–60 mins?

🪜 Pain Relief Progression Ladder

Tier Method Preference & Details
Tier 1 Comfort & Movement Rhythmic breathing, birthing ball, TENS machine on lower back, partner counter-pressure.
Tier 2 Inhaled Gas & Air Entonox available as contractions intensify. (Keep lip balm on hand).
Tier 3 Medical Analgesia Open to discussing Diamorphine/Pethidine or Epidural on Delivery Suite if labour is prolonged or pain becomes unmanageable.

🤰 Labour & Pushing

  • Monitoring: Intermittent Doppler monitoring preferred to keep mum mobile.
  • Examinations: Minimal vaginal checks with prior consent.
  • Positions: Freedom for upright, gravity-assisted positions (all-fours, kneeling over bed head, side-lying). No flat-on-back pushing.
  • Perineum: Warm compresses; natural tear preferred over episiotomy unless baby is in distress.

👶 The Golden Hour (Third Stage)

  • Placenta: Active management (Syntometrine injection).
  • Cord Clamping: Delayed Cord Clamping (2–3 mins until pulsing stops).
  • Cord Cutting: Partner wishes to cut the umbilical cord.
  • Skin-to-Skin: Immediate uninterrupted skin-to-skin for 1+ hours (defer weighing/wiping).
  • Vitamin K: Consented single intramuscular injection for baby.

⚠️ Contingencies: Gentle C-Section Protocol

If transfer to theatre becomes necessary: Partner seated right next to mum's head throughout; our music playing in theatre; surgical screen lowered at delivery so we can see baby born; partner announces sex; immediate skin-to-skin directly on mum's chest (or partner's bare chest if mum is recovering).

Phased Care Plan (Days, Weeks & Months)

Parent-baby roadmap for the first 72 hours, 4-week fourth trimester, and months 1–3.

🐣 Phase 1: The First 72 Hours (Homecoming)

Mum’s "Recovery Nest"

Set up mum's station in the living room and bedroom so she rarely needs to stand up:

  • 2L insulated water bottle with straw (hydration drives milk supply).
  • High-protein snacks (nuts, flapjacks, cheese).
  • Phone charger with 3m cable + lip balm.
  • Nursing pillow and silver cups.

NHS Home Visits

  • Day 1–2: Community Midwife home visit (checks mum's bleeding/perineum, baby jaundice, cord stump).
  • Day 5: Midwife heel prick (Guthrie blood spot test) + baby weight check. (Partner: hold baby skin-to-skin during test).
  • Day 3–4: Normal "Baby blues" hormonal drop. Be patient, kind, and reassuring.

🌙 Phase 2: Weeks 1 to 4 (Split Night Shifts)

Protecting uninterrupted 4–5 hour sleep blocks is our primary operational rule.

The "Feed & Pass" System:
Shift 1: 9:00 PM – 2:00 AM (Partner On-Duty)

Mum sleeps uninterrupted in spare room. Partner soothes baby in living room. When baby hungry, partner brings baby to mum in bed to nurse. Partner immediately takes baby back to burp, change, and settle.

Shift 2: 2:00 AM – 7:00 AM (Mum On-Duty)

Partner sleeps 5 solid hours undisturbed in spare room. Mum manages feeds and Next2Me crib. Partner wakes up at 7am to make coffee and breakfast.

Chore Triage

Only 3 household rules exist in month 1:

  1. Baby is fed, warm, and safe.
  2. Mum is fed, hydrated, and resting.
  3. Dishes and baby laundry keep rotating.

Daily 45-Min Partner Walk

Partner takes baby in pram/carrier outside every afternoon. Baby sleeps, partner gets exercise, and mum gets 45 minutes of guaranteed silent house time to shower or nap.

🌿 Phase 3: Months 1 to 3 (Routines & Milestones)

Wake Windows Guide

Start winding down at the first yawn or eye-rub:

  • 0–4 Weeks: 45–60 minutes awake.
  • 4–8 Weeks: 60–75 minutes awake.
  • 8–12 Weeks: 75–90 minutes awake.

NHS Milestone Schedule

  • Day 10–14: Midwife hands over to Health Visitor.
  • 6–8 Weeks: GP full maternal & baby physical check.
  • 8 Weeks: First NHS vaccines (6-in-1, Rotavirus, MenB + Calpol!).

  • 16 Weeks: Third NHS immunisations.

🩹 Physical Healing Toolkit

  • Upside-down Peri Bottle: Squirt warm water while peeing to prevent stinging. Pat dry gently.
  • Cooling Padsicles: Maternity pads with witch hazel chilled in fridge/freezer for swelling.
  • High-Waisted Briefs: Sits well above any pubic soreness or C-section incision.
  • Lochia Bleeding: Normal for 3–6 weeks. Red flag: Soaking >1 pad/hour or passing golf ball-sized clots (call NNUH triage).

🧠 Daily "1 to 10" Emotional Check-In

Partner asks every evening while baby is settled:

"How is your head today from 1 to 10?"
  • 1–4 (Struggling): Partner takes over chores and baby entirely; focus on rest, food, warmth.
  • 5–7 (Treading water): Normal postpartum exhaustion. Hugs, tea, empathy.
  • 8–10 (Thriving): Celebrate the day!

🚨 Breast Care & Mastitis Red Flags

Engorgement Protocol (Days 3–5):

Warm shower/compress 2 mins before feed to trigger letdown. Cold gel packs or chilled cabbage leaves for 10 mins after feed to soothe tissue swelling.

Mastitis Red Flags:

Hot red wedge/streak on breast + flu-like aches + fever >38°C. Action: Do NOT stop nursing, take 400mg Ibuprofen, and call GP / NHS 111 for antibiotics if not improving within 12–24h.

Practical Support & Visitor Guide

A practical plan to handle visitors, put them to work, and get them to leave gracefully.

🛑 The Three Golden Rules

1. Hospital is for Us

Zero hospital visitors at NNUH. Family and friends meet baby once settled at home.

2. Wash Hands on Entry

Warm soapy water at the sink before anyone touches or holds baby.

3. Zero Kissing

No kissing baby on face or hands (prevents neonatal cold sores/herpes and RSV).

🛠️ How to Put Visitors to Work

Give willing guests a specific job:

  • The Admission Ticket: "Could you pick up a pint of milk and loaf of bread on your way?"
  • The Shower Sitter: Hand baby to guest for 20 mins so mum can take an uninterrupted hot shower.
  • The Dishwasher Swap: "Feel free to put the kettle on! While it boils, could you empty the dishwasher?"

🚪 How to Get Them to Leave

Cap visits at 45 minutes max. Partner acts as timekeeper:

  • Mum's Exit: "Baby is showing hunger cues, so I'm taking them upstairs into the quiet for a feed and nap!"
  • Partner's Sweep: "Right, let me grab your coats and walk you to the door before the next cluster feed!"
